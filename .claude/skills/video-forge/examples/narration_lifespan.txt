## HOOK
Methuselah lived nine hundred and sixty-nine years.
And if you add up the numbers in Genesis…
he died in the very same year the Flood came.
Adam lived nine hundred and thirty years.
Noah, nine hundred and fifty.
Today, almost no one reaches one hundred.
So either the Bible got its numbers wrong…
or something happened to the human race.
Something we have forgotten.
!DID THEY REALLY LIVE 900 YEARS?
## THE LIST
It is all written in Genesis chapter five.
A family tree that reads like a list of impossible birthdays.
Adam, nine hundred and thirty.
Seth, nine hundred and twelve.
Enosh, nine hundred and five.
Jared, nine hundred and sixty-two.
And Methuselah, the oldest man who ever lived.
Every name comes with an exact age.
The age he became a father.
And the age he died.
This is not written like a fairy tale.
It is written like a record.
So what is going on?
## MISTRANSLATION
The most popular answer online is simple.
It is a mistranslation.
They were counting months, not years.
Nine hundred and thirty months is about seventy-seven years.
Perfectly normal.
Problem solved?
Not so fast.
Look at Enoch.
Genesis says Enoch became a father at sixty-five.
If those are months…
Enoch was five years old.
And Mahalalel?
Also a father at sixty-five.
A five-year-old dad.
The month theory doesn't fix the problem.
It creates a bigger one.
And the Hebrew word used here is shanah.
It means year.
The same word used for every other year in the Bible.
!Myth number one. Busted.
## COPIED MYTH
So maybe the numbers were borrowed.
Skeptics point to an ancient clay tablet from Mesopotamia.
The Sumerian King List.
It names eight kings who ruled before a great flood.
And their reigns are insane.
One king ruled for twenty-eight thousand, eight hundred years.
Another, for thirty-six thousand.
Together, those eight kings ruled for over two hundred and forty thousand years.
And then the flood came.
After the flood, the reigns suddenly shrink.
Sound familiar?
Two completely different cultures.
Both remember long-lived ancestors.
Both remember a flood.
And both remember lifespans collapsing after it.
But notice the difference.
The Sumerian numbers are fantasy.
The Bible's numbers are strangely restrained.
Not thousands of years.
Not tens of thousands.
Under one thousand, every single time.
Maybe this is not one story copying another.
Maybe it is two memories of the same event.
## THE WORLD BEFORE
So what was different back then?
Genesis describes a world we would not recognize.
Waters above the sky.
The fountains of the great deep, still sealed.
Some believe a thick layer of water vapor once shielded the earth.
Less radiation. A gentler climate.
Others believe the first humans simply had near-perfect genes.
Fewer mutations. Bodies that could repair themselves for centuries.
These ideas are debated, even among believers.
But one thing is not debated.
The numbers change after the Flood.
## THE COLLAPSE
Watch what happens.
Noah, nine hundred and fifty.
His son Shem, six hundred.
A few generations later, four hundred.
Then two hundred and thirty.
Abraham, one hundred and seventy-five.
Joseph, one hundred and ten.
Moses, one hundred and twenty.
And a few centuries later, the psalm says…
"The years of our life are seventy, or even by reason of strength eighty."
That is not random.
It is a curve.
A steady, downward decline.
Exactly what you would expect if something in humanity was breaking down.
## 120
And there is one more verse that changes everything.
Genesis six, verse three.
Before the Flood, God says…
"My Spirit shall not abide in man forever… his days shall be one hundred and twenty years."
Many read this as a countdown to the Flood.
Others read it as a new limit on human life.
Now here is the strange part.
The oldest person ever verified by modern science…
was a French woman named Jeanne Calment.
She died at one hundred and twenty-two.
In thousands of years of records…
no one has broken the limit by more than a breath.
## THE VERDICT
So, did they really live nine hundred years?
Mistranslation?
The text says years, and the math breaks if it does not.
Copied from a myth?
The Bible's account is far more restrained than the myths around it.
Literal?
The text certainly presents itself that way.
And the steady decline after the Flood fits that story.
But the real question is not how long they lived.
It is why they died at all.
## THE ANSWER
In the beginning, death was not part of the plan.
Adam was made to live.
The tree of life stood in the middle of the garden.
But when sin entered the world, death came with it.
Nine hundred and thirty years was not a superpower.
It was what remained of something much greater.
And the Bible ends where it began.
With a tree of life.
And a promise.
"Death shall be no more."
!Not nine hundred years. Forever.
