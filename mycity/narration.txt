## HOOK
My city sits at the bottom of a labyrinth.
Every Sunday, somebody new wakes up on the steps of our fountain…
!and nobody — not one of us — remembers where we came from.
## WHO WE ARE
See, my city is called Middle.
It's small. Forty-one houses, one bakery, one bell tower, and a fountain that never runs dry.
Around it, on every side, there's a wall.
Big, grey, covered in ivy, taller than the bell tower.
And in that wall there are four gates — north, south, east and west.
!Behind every gate… the labyrinth.
I've lived here for three hundred and twelve Sundays.
I know that because I scratch a line on my bedroom wall every week.
My name is Kettle.
Not because my parents called me that.
Because when I woke up on the fountain steps, the first thing I saw was a copper kettle sitting next to me, still warm.
That's how it works here. You get named after the first thing you see.
My best friend is called Pebble.
The man who runs the bakery is called Ladder.
!And the oldest person in the city, the one who decides everything, is called Old Brass — because he woke up staring at the brass bell above the north gate.
## THE RULES
Middle has three rules. And everybody learns them on their first day.
Rule one: when the bell rings three times, you go inside. No matter what.
Rule two: you never cross a gate alone.
!Rule three: you never, ever follow a voice in the labyrinth. Not even if it knows your name.
When I was new, I thought the rules were silly.
!Then I saw my first Hollow.
## THE HOLLOWS
It was my fourth week.
The bell rang three times, and everyone ran inside, slamming doors and pulling curtains.
I was slow. I stood in the street, looking at the north gate, because I could hear something.
A soft, scraping sound. Like someone dragging a coat across stone.
Then it came out of the dark.
Tall. Thin. Way too many joints in its arms.
Its body looked like an old grey coat with nobody inside, and where its face should have been, there was just a smooth, empty oval.
No eyes. No mouth. Nothing.
It turned that empty face towards me.
And it said my name.
!"Kettle." In a voice that sounded exactly… like mine.
Pebble yanked me through her door just before it reached the fountain.
We sat on her floor until morning, listening to it walk up and down the street, saying names.
Ladder. Brass. Pebble. Kettle.
!When the sun came up, it was gone. And all that was left was a long, wet trail leading back to the north gate.
## THE CHALKERS
After that night, I wanted to know what was out there. So I joined the Chalkers.
The Chalkers are the bravest people in Middle.
Every morning, when the gates open, they go into the labyrinth in pairs, carrying sticks of white chalk.
They walk the corridors, and they draw arrows on the walls, so that one day, somebody can find the way out.
Every evening, they come back and draw what they found on a giant map that covers the whole floor of the bell tower.
The map is beautiful. It's also impossible.
Because the arrows don't stay where you draw them.
You draw an arrow pointing left on Monday… and on Wednesday it points right.
You draw a corridor on the map… and next week the corridor is a dead end with ivy growing on it.
!The labyrinth doesn't move. Old Brass says the labyrinth corrects itself.
Pebble and I chalked together for two hundred Sundays.
We found a garden full of stone benches.
A staircase that went down into water.
A corridor where every wall was covered with tiny scratched lines, thousands of them, just like the ones in my bedroom.
!And once, deep in the west, we found a copper kettle, lying in the dirt. Cold.
!I didn't tell anyone about that.
## THE NEW ONES
Meanwhile, every Sunday, somebody new arrived.
A boy named Button. A girl named Lantern.
A tall, quiet guy named Umbrella, who cried for three days and then became the best baker Ladder ever had.
They always wake up the same way. On the fountain steps. Clean clothes. Empty pockets. And one object next to them.
They never remember anything. Not their family. Not their home.
Not even what their own face looks like until they see it in the fountain.
But here's what bothered me.
The city never gets bigger. Forty-one houses.
Every Sunday someone new arrives… and somehow, there's always one empty bed waiting for them.
I asked Old Brass about that once. He didn't answer.
!He just looked at the north gate for a long time, and then he told me to go home before the bell.
## WHAT PEBBLE FOUND
!Then, on my three-hundredth Sunday, Pebble didn't come back from the labyrinth.
She'd gone in with Button that morning. In the evening, only Button came out.
White as chalk, shaking, saying they got separated at a crossing in the east.
That night, the bell rang three times, and I sat at my window, staring at the east gate.
Around midnight, the scraping started.
A Hollow came out. And it stopped right under my window.
!And it said, very softly, "Kettle. It's me."
I didn't open the window. I'm not proud of it.
!But I followed rule three.
The next morning, I went into the labyrinth alone, breaking rule two.
I followed Pebble's chalk arrows all the way east, until the arrows stopped and her handwriting started.
She'd written on the walls. Short sentences, getting messier and messier.
"The Hollows aren't from the labyrinth."
"They're from Middle."
"Count the beds."
!"Go to the centre."
## THE CENTRE
So I went.
Twelve Sundays in a row, I slipped into the labyrinth before sunrise and came back right before the bell.
I didn't draw arrows anymore. I followed the scratched lines on the walls instead.
Because I'd figured something out. The lines weren't random. They were tally marks, like mine.
!And they got denser… the closer you got to the middle of the maze.
On my three hundred and twelfth Sunday — today — I found it.
A round courtyard. No ivy. No chalk.
In the middle stood a single, enormous door.
And next to it, sitting on a stone bench, was a Hollow.
Just sitting there. Not scraping. Not moving.
When I came closer, it lifted its empty face.
!And in its long grey hand, it held a stick of white chalk. Pebble's chalk.
"You counted the beds," it said. In Pebble's voice. Her real voice, this time.
!And then she told me everything.
## THE REVEAL
Middle isn't a city that new people get sent to.
!It's a city that people get sent back to.
Every week, one person from Middle reaches the centre.
Every week, one person walks through that door.
And the door does two things. It takes your memory… and it takes your face.
Most people come out the other side as Hollows.
!Empty. Wandering the labyrinth, calling the names of everyone they loved, because names are the last thing you lose.
But some people — the door gives a second chance.
It cleans them up. Puts new clothes on them.
Lays them on the fountain steps on a Sunday morning, with one object next to them.
!The first thing they saw before they went through.
Button was a Chalker before.
Umbrella was a baker before — that's why he was so good at it.
And forty-one houses is all Middle ever needed, because every new one… is an old one.
!That's why there's always an empty bed.
That's why I found a copper kettle deep in the west.
!I'd carried it into the labyrinth once. A long, long time ago.
And Pebble? She went through the door.
!She came out on the wrong side.
"Don't go through," she said. "Go home. Ring the bell. Tell Old Brass you know."
So I walked home. I rang the bell.
And when Old Brass opened his door, he wasn't surprised at all.
!He just nodded, very slowly, and said: "You're the fourth time you've figured it out, Kettle."
## ENDING
My city sits at the bottom of a labyrinth.
!Every Sunday, somebody new wakes up on the steps of our fountain.
!Next Sunday… I think I'm going to tell them the truth.
Or maybe… I'm going to find out what it's like to wake up with a kettle next to me.
!Again.
