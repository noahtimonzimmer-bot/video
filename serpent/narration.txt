## HOOK
Everyone knows the story.
A garden. A tree. A woman. And a talking snake.
But what if the creature in Eden was never a snake at all?
What if the Bible has been telling us something much stranger…
and we have been picturing it wrong for thousands of years?
Today, we are going back to the very first pages of Scripture.
And by the end, you will never read Genesis the same way again.
!THE SERPENT IN EDEN.
## THE GARDEN
In the beginning, God planted a garden in the east, in Eden.
A river flowed out of Eden to water the garden.
And from there it split into four rivers.
Trees of every kind grew there, pleasant to the eye and good for food.
In the middle stood two trees.
The tree of life.
And the tree of the knowledge of good and evil.
God placed the man in the garden to work it and to keep it.
And He gave him one command.
"You may freely eat of every tree of the garden…
but of the tree of the knowledge of good and evil you shall not eat."
Then God made the woman.
And for a short time, everything was exactly as it should be.
## THE QUESTION
Then, in Genesis chapter three, something walks into the story.
"Now the serpent was more crafty than any beast of the field which the Lord God had made."
It speaks.
It reasons.
It knows exactly what God said.
And it knows how to twist it.
"Did God actually say, you shall not eat of any tree in the garden?"
Snakes do not talk.
Snakes do not argue about theology.
So what is this thing?
## THE WORD
To answer that, we have to look at the original Hebrew.
The word translated as serpent is nachash.
And here is where it gets strange.
The same three letters appear in other words across the Old Testament.
In Genesis forty-four, Joseph's silver cup is the cup he uses for divination.
The verb for divination there is nachash.
In other places, the related word nechoshet means bronze.
Shining. Gleaming. Polished metal.
So the word that describes this creature is connected to three ideas.
A serpent.
A diviner who knows hidden things.
And something that shines.
Some scholars believe the author chose this word on purpose.
Not just a snake.
A shining one.
## THE SHINING SERPENTS
And that idea is not as wild as it sounds.
In Numbers twenty-one, the people of Israel are bitten in the desert by fiery serpents.
In Hebrew, they are called seraphim.
Burning ones.
God tells Moses to make a serpent of bronze and lift it up on a pole.
A bronze serpent. A nachash of nechoshet.
Now turn to Isaiah chapter six.
Isaiah sees the Lord on His throne.
And above Him stand… seraphim.
The same word. Burning ones.
Heavenly beings, standing in the very presence of God.
So in the Bible, serpent language and divine beings are closer than most people realize.
## EZEKIEL 28
And then there is one passage that changes everything.
Ezekiel chapter twenty-eight.
It begins as a lament over the king of Tyre, a rich island city of ships and trade.
But then the words go far beyond any human king.
"You were in Eden, the garden of God.
Every precious stone was your covering.
You were an anointed guardian cherub.
You were on the holy mountain of God.
You were blameless in your ways from the day you were created…
till unrighteousness was found in you."
A being in Eden.
Covered in shining stones.
A cherub on the mountain of God.
Who fell because of his pride.
"I cast you to the ground."
## THE CURSE
Now go back to the curse in Genesis three.
"On your belly you shall go, and dust you shall eat all the days of your life."
Many readers take this to mean the snake lost its legs.
But look closely.
The Bible never says the serpent had legs.
In Scripture, eating dust and lying in the dust are pictures of total humiliation.
In Psalm seventy-two, the enemies of the king lick the dust.
In Micah seven, the nations lick the dust like a serpent.
So the curse may not be about biology at all.
It is about a proud being brought down to the lowest place there is.
From the mountain of God… to the dust of the earth.
## THE ANSWER
So who was the serpent?
The Bible does not leave us guessing.
In Revelation chapter twelve, John writes it plainly.
"That ancient serpent, who is called the devil and Satan, the deceiver of the whole world."
And Paul warns that Satan disguises himself as an angel of light.
An angel of light.
A shining one.
## TRUTH OR MYTH
So let's separate what the Bible confirms from what is speculation.
Confirmed.
The serpent was not just an animal.
Scripture itself identifies it as Satan.
Confirmed.
It was intelligent, it spoke, and it deceived.
Not confirmed.
That the serpent once walked on legs.
The text simply does not say it.
And possible, but debated.
That nachash points to a shining, divine being.
Many scholars hold this view, and the language supports it.
But others read it as Satan speaking through a real serpent.
Either way, the conclusion is the same.
The enemy in Eden was no ordinary snake.
## THE PROMISE
But the story does not end with the serpent.
In the very same chapter, God makes a promise.
"I will put enmity between you and the woman, and between your offspring and her offspring.
He shall bruise your head, and you shall bruise his heel."
One day, a son of the woman would crush the serpent's head.
Christians have read this as the first announcement of the gospel.
And thousands of years later, Jesus said these words.
"As Moses lifted up the serpent in the wilderness, so must the Son of Man be lifted up."
The bronze serpent on the pole…
pointed to the cross.
The serpent struck the heel.
But the head was crushed.
!The shining one fell. The Son was lifted up.
And that is the real story of the serpent in Eden.
